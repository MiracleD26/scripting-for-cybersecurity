1. Current accounts are alice, bob, eve, david.

This suggests the attacker clearly knew these were valid usernames.
They targeted, active accounts from the current user directory, which also indicates that the attacker
had access to the user list.

2. Targeted names that are legacy accounts found only in the old backup are, admin, root and backup.

If they still existed a root would be the most dangerous because,
you full unrestricted access to everything.

3. No david is the only disabled account but had 0 accepted logins.

Accepted logins tells you that someone successfully signed in as a user with a password.

It doesn't prove whether the attacker is still active or who the attacker is....

4. The accounts that have both plaintext credentials on the workstation and successful logins were,
alice and bob.


Alice had 4 different internal addresses that she logged in with and bob had 3.
That's worth reporting because that means either 1, multiple PC's have been compromised or 2,
the attacker is attacking by, changing IP addresses by using a VPN or attacking from different
locations.
